# Constant Reader

Everything the literary press reviewed in the last ten years, scored out of ten against one reader's taste profile.

Live at https://beingmtm714.github.io/constant-reader/

Eighty-four publications sit in the registry and fifty-four are enabled: Publishers Weekly, Book Marks, the NYRB, the LRB, LARB, the New Yorker, the Guardian, the NYT Book Review, the Atlantic, the Paris Review, the Chicago and Cleveland Reviews of Books, the LA Times, Full Stop, Electric Literature, The Millions, Kirkus, Bookforum, Public Books, 4Columns and the rest. Thirty-seven of them are review sources whose text can move a score. The others — publisher catalogues and the New Books Network — establish that a book exists and supply its metadata, and never touch a score, because jacket copy and author interviews never say the prose is the weakness.

**The unit is the book, not the review.** Three publications on the same novel is one row carrying three mentions, scored in a single pass over all three texts.

The feed does not filter on taste. Everything in the window is there, scored, whether it is close to the profile or nowhere near it. Anything at 7.0 or above is tagged recommended.

## What the score is

Seven weighted dimensions read out of review prose by keyword: prose register (24 points), historical causation (20), formal ambition (18), subject engine (14), scale and sprawl (10), independence of the press (8) and tonal signature (6). Every dimension shows the terms it fired on, which is what makes a wrong score visible as a wrong score. Treat a number as triage, not as a reading of the book.

A book is scored only on the dimensions the reviews actually spoke to, renormalised over them. Below 30 of the 100 points of weight that is thin evidence, and the total is capped at 6.9 so two dimensions cannot manufacture a top score. Where a review names the prose itself as the book's weakness, the total is capped at 6.0 whatever the architecture is worth.

No score and no description is generated by a model. The descriptions are the publications' own standfirsts. The one place a model is used is Search, below.

## The seven screens

**For you** is the day's edit: one spotlight, what the shortlist leans toward, a four-book shelf and the newest reviews. **Review feed** is every reviewed title, scored and unscored, with search, sort and filters. **All books** is the archive, including the books no critic has reached yet. **Saved** is the shelf. **Taste** shows what your saves have taught it. **Profile** is where you set the terms. A seventh, the profile builder, has no slot in the navigation because nobody arrives at it twice.

## Your ordering

The words are shared. What they are worth is not. The **Profile** screen re-scores any band, re-weights any dimension, switches off any rule or penalty, and the feed re-ranks in the browser with no rebuild. The seven weights must total 100 and wait for Save; a band score or a switch takes effect the moment it changes. Your saves tune a score by at most 0.8 out of ten on top of that, and can never overrule a rule.

## Search

Search reads a question in your own words. Keyword matching runs in the browser first and answers at once. When the Ask worker is configured, Claude then reads the question for meaning: it suggests the words a critic would have used, the browser searches the archive again with them, and Claude picks the books that genuinely answer from the forty or so that turns up, with a line on why each one does. Your profile then orders those answers the same bounded way it orders everything else. If the worker cannot answer, the keyword results stay.

The filter field on Review feed and All books is a different thing: it narrows the list in front of you by title, author, publisher or critic.

## Privacy

Saving, passing and every preference are written to this browser's local storage, and the app opens, ranks and re-scores with no network and no account. A search question, and the titles and standfirsts of the books it might match, go to the Ask worker and on to Anthropic to be read for meaning. Your profile, saves and passes never do.

Signing in is optional and off unless you ask for it. It is Google sign-in only, and it syncs your saves, your passes and your profile changes to a document in your own Google account and nowhere else. The Firebase SDK is not even fetched until the first time you tap Sign in. Filters, sort order and the rest stay on the device that set them: they describe a browser, not a reader.

## Where it comes from

Everything is in this repository: the crawler and scorer (`bin/`, `lib/`, `sources.json`), the taste profile (`profile/`) and the app (`web/`). A GitHub Actions workflow crawls, tests and deploys it to Pages three times a week. `SCORING.md` explains the scores, `ENGINEERING.md` the system and `DESIGN.md` the app.
